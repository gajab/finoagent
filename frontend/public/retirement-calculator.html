Why a Monte Carlo retirement calculator beats a simple one

Most retirement planning tools assume one fixed return every year — say 7% — and draw a smooth line. Real markets don't work that way. A bad run of returns early in retirement (sequence-of-returns risk) can drain a portfolio that a fixed-return calculator would call perfectly safe. Our retirement calculator instead samples jointly from 1975–2025 stock, bond, and inflation history, preserving how those move together, and reports a success probability with percentile outcome bands rather than a single falsely-precise number.

Estimate your FIRE age (Financial Independence, Retire Early)

The built-in FIRE calculator answers the question every early-retirement planner asks: how soon can I stop working? It sweeps candidate retirement ages and finds the earliest one where your plan still clears a high success target, then shows how four levers change that date:

  • As planned — your earliest FIRE age on current inputs.
  • Growth portfolio — a higher stock allocation and its effect on the date.
  • Leaner spending — how trimming expenses accelerates independence.
  • Both combined — the aggressive early-retirement path.

Each scenario has a one-click Apply so you can fold it straight into your plan and re-run — a real simulated comparison, not generic advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free retirement calculator?

One that uses Monte Carlo simulation over real market history rather than a single fixed return, and that accounts for taxes, RMDs, and Social Security. FinoAgent's retirement calculator does all three, free and without a login.

How does the FIRE estimate work?

It projects your savings forward with contributions and market growth, then finds the earliest age your portfolio can sustain your spending at a high success rate — and shows how allocation and spending changes move that date.

When should I claim Social Security?

It depends on longevity, other income, taxes, and spousal benefits. The calculator's claim-age optimizer compares ages 62–70 on the same simulations so you can see the trade-off directly.

Are Roth conversions worth it in retirement?

Often, especially in lower-income years before RMDs and Social Security begin. The Roth conversion planner sweeps bracket ceilings and age windows to find a schedule that lowers lifetime taxes.
