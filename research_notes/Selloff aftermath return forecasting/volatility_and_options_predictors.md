# Volatility- and options-based predictors of short-horizon (1–20 trading day) US index returns and drawdowns; risk-neutral vs physical distributions

Scope note: these notes combine (a) the peer-reviewed and central-bank literature, (b) industry or blog sources, each flagged as such, and (c) an **own computation** on daily ^GSPC, ^VIX and ^VIX3M closes from Yahoo Finance (1990-01-02 → 2026-10-16, 9,266 days; VIX3M from 2006-07-17). The computation is **in-sample and descriptive**. It uses overlapping windows and the price index without dividends (about 0.15% per 20 days, negligible next to σ). Method: VIX σ is scaled to each horizon as `VIX·√(calendar days/365)`; the cone is lognormal with median `−σ²/2`; "outside ±1σ" means |log r| > σ (31.7% under the model); P10/P90 coverage should each be 10%. Stress subsets are clustered: VIX≥40 is 208 days but only **13 independent episodes** (1998-08, 2001-09, 2002-07, 2002-09, 2008-09, 2010-05, 2011-08, 2011-09, 2015-08, 2020-02, 2020-06, 2020-10, 2025-04). "5-day return ≤ −5%" is 181 days in 46 episodes, and VIX/VIX3M > 1.1 is 133 days in 18 episodes. Data: [Yahoo ^VIX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX/), [Yahoo ^GSPC](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EGSPC/), [Yahoo ^VIX3M](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX3M/).

## Q1. VIX level and VIX changes as predictors of subsequent S&P 500 returns (Giot 2005; Banerjee-Doran-Peterson 2007; Whaley; Copeland & Copeland)

### Takeaway
VIX *level by itself* is a weak, statistically fragile predictor of 1–3-month index returns in rigorous tests (Johnson; Bekaert-Hoerova). The "buy extreme VIX" pattern (Giot) appears in descriptive studies and in our in-sample data: mean 20-day return is about +2.1% when VIX ≥ 30, versus +0.5–0.6% when VIX < 20. But it rests on about 13 independent ≥40 episodes, its dispersion is very large, and it has no established out-of-sample record. Treat it as a **conditional-distribution shift (higher mean and much wider spread), not a timing signal.**

### Cited Findings
- Giot (2005, *J. Portfolio Mgmt* 31(3):92–100), working-paper abstract: rising VIX/VXN coincides with negative same-period returns (the "fear factor"). *Very high* implied-vol levels statistically signal short-term index *increases*, while average-to-moderately-high levels lead to unfavourable mean-variance returns. The author therefore suggests waiting for extreme readings before buying oversold markets. Sample: S&P 100 / Nasdaq 100. The published version's numbers were not verified. — [UCLouvain WP / IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cor/louvrp/1848.html); [UCLouvain repository](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/bitstreams/995d05e0-4d85-40d7-8ea7-3391a0e4365e/download)
- Banerjee, Doran & Peterson (2007, *J. Banking & Finance* 31(10):3183–3199) test VIX levels and deviations of VIX from its short-term mean as predictors of 22- and 44-trading-day returns on size/value/beta-sorted portfolios, June 1986–June 2005. A thesis literature review cites the paper as finding a *positive* implied-vol → future-return relation. Bali & Hovakimian (2009) find little predictive power. The exact coefficients were not retrieved. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v31y2007i10p3183-3199.html); [CXO summary (industry)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/vix-as-an-indicator-for-different-kinds-of-portfolios); [Lund thesis review](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9222242/file/9222251.pdf)
- Related: DeLisle, Doran & Peterson (2011, *J. Futures Markets*): sensitivity to VIX innovations is negatively priced when volatility is rising and unrelated when it is falling. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v31y2011i1p34-54.html)
- Copeland & Copeland (1999, *FAJ* 55(2):73–81): *changes* in VIX are statistically significant leading indicators of *daily* returns. After VIX rises, large-cap and value beat small-cap and growth, and the pattern reverses after VIX falls. This is a style/size rotation result, not index direction. A replication on 1990–2008 data finds significance only for holding periods of ≥30 days. A Canadian MVX replication found the value/growth leg works but the size leg is not robust. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/1999/market-timing-style-and-size-rotation-using-the-vix); [Financial Services Review replication](https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/view/4689); [SFU thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/750)
- Whaley (2009, "Understanding the VIX," *JPM* 35(3):98–105): a descriptive study, Jan 1986–Oct 2008. VIX mean-reverts and is *asymmetric*: it rises more when the market falls than it falls when the market rises, attributed to put-buying demand. It is a contemporaneous relation, not a forecast. — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/understanding-the-vix-whaley-robert/10003852143); [CXO summary (industry)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/history-and-meaning-of-vix/)
- Johnson (2011 Stanford job-market draft of the later JFQA paper), daily 1996–2010: "by itself, the one-month VIX has no predictive power for future one- and three-month returns, statistically insignificant power for future six-month returns, and some predictive power for one-year returns." — [Johnson 2011 draft (Univ. of Houston seminar PDF)](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/seminars/Johnson_020712.pdf)
- Bekaert & Hoerova (NBER w18995, published *J. Econometrics* 2014), monthly 1990–2007: "the squared VIX itself fails to predict stock returns." The predictive content of VIX for returns sits in its variance-premium component, not its conditional-variance component. — [NBER w18995](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18995.pdf)
- **Own computation (in-sample, 1990–2026)**: mean 20-day forward log return by VIX bucket is <15: +0.57% (P(up) 65.6%); 15–20: +0.53%; 20–25: +0.37%; 25–30: +0.93%; 30–40: +2.13% (P(up) 73.7%, n=528 days, 38 episodes); ≥40: +2.16% (P(up) 61.5%, n=208 days, 13 episodes). The 5-day means rise from +0.10% (<15) to +0.68% (≥40). After a 5-day drop of ≤ −5% (46 episodes), the next 5 days averaged +1.15% (P(up) 64.6%) and the next 20 days +2.5%. After a drawdown of ≤ −10% from the 252-day high, the 20-day mean is only +0.34%. — data: [Yahoo ^VIX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX/), [Yahoo ^GSPC](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EGSPC/)

### Inferences
- The mean shift after VIX spikes is consistent with a time-varying risk premium (compensation is highest when fear is highest). It is also consistent with the volatility-asymmetry mechanism (Whaley): spikes coincide with falls, then mean-revert. Both readings imply higher *mean* and higher *variance* together. A monitor should therefore display the shift as "wider fan with an upward-shifted median," never as a directional call.
- **Folklore flag:** "VIX > 30/40 = buy" is supported only descriptively and in-sample. It rests on few independent episodes and lacks any out-of-sample test in the peer-reviewed literature found here. The rigorous papers (Johnson, Bekaert-Hoerova) find raw VIX weak at 1–3 months.
- A drawdown-conditioned subset (≤ −10%) has a *lower* 20-day mean than the "sharp 5-day drop" subset. Washed-out sharp drops behave differently from grinding bear markets, so analog conditioning should use the *speed* of the decline, not just its depth.

### Gaps
- Published effect sizes for Giot (2005) and Banerjee-Doran-Peterson (2007) could not be retrieved; only abstracts and summaries.
- No peer-reviewed out-of-sample test of the "VIX spike → positive 1–20-day return" rule was found.
- MOVE (bond implied vol) as a predictor of equity returns: no peer-reviewed evidence was located in this session.

## Q2. VIX term structure (VIX vs VIX3M, backwardation, futures basis): does inversion predict returns or realized volatility?

### Takeaway
The term-structure slope chiefly predicts **returns on variance assets** (variance swaps, VIX futures, straddles), meaning it measures how large the variance risk premium is. It does not reliably predict next-month S&P 500 returns (Johnson). Spot-VIX changes are not forecast by the VIX-futures basis either (Simon & Campasano). In our data, mild backwardation (VIX/VIX3M 1.0–1.1) coincided with above-average 5–20-day returns. **Extreme backwardation (>1.1) coincided with negative mean returns, a 20-day P10 of −21%, and realized vol *above* VIX.** Within VIX≥25, inversion adds no bullish information over VIX level alone.

### Cited Findings
- Johnson (2017, *JFQA* 52(6):2461–2490, "Risk Premia and the VIX Term Structure"). The term structure's shape reflects the *price of variance risk*, not expected VIX changes, which rejects the expectations hypothesis. SLOPE (the 2nd principal component) "captures nearly all of that information" and forecasts excess returns on synthetic S&P 500 variance swaps, VIX futures and S&P 500 straddles at all maturities. It is incremental to other VRP proxies. The data are six VIX-style maturities (1–12 months), 1996–2013. Per an industry summary, a *lower* (inverted) slope predicts *higher* variance-asset returns. — [Cambridge/JFQA abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/risk-premia-and-the-vix-term-structure/56572D1F060448571BD8F597C732D9C3); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v52y2017i06p2461-2490_00.html); [CXO summary (industry)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/vix-term-structure-slope-and-variance-asset-future-returns/)
- Johnson 2011 draft (equity-return version), daily 1996–2010, Newey-West SEs. The full VIX term structure adds incremental R² of **1.99% (1-month, *not* significant), 5.16% (3-month), 6.60% (6-month) and 3.73% (12-month)**, and predicts next-quarter excess S&P 500 returns with a **5.2% adjusted R²**. The result survives controls for the BTZ VRP, dividend yield, net payout yield and the Cochrane-Piazzesi factor, and survives excluding Oct 2008–Jun 2009. The power comes from the **4th principal component** (a "lightning bolt" shape), *not* the level/slope/curvature components. No out-of-sample test is reported. — [Johnson 2011 draft PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/seminars/Johnson_020712.pdf)
- Same draft: the term structure is downward-sloping when 1-month VIX is high and upward-sloping when it is low, so inversion is largely a proxy for a high VIX level. — [Johnson 2011 draft PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/seminars/Johnson_020712.pdf)
- Simon & Campasano (WP 2012/2013, "The VIX Futures Basis: Evidence and Trading Strategies"; its listing as *J. Derivatives* 2014 was not verified). Over 2006–2011 the basis does **not** forecast spot-VIX changes but **does** forecast VIX-futures returns, i.e., it is a harvestable risk premium. The strategy sells (buys) the front future in contango (backwardation) when the daily roll exceeds 0.10 points, holds 5 days, and hedges with E-minis. — [EFMA 2013 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2013-Reading/papers/EFMA2013_0164_fullpaper.pdf); [CXO summary (industry)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/timing-and-hedging-the-roll-return-for-vix-futures/)
- Cheng (2019, *RFS* 32(1):180–227, "The VIX Premium"). Ex-ante VIX-futures premiums **fall or stay flat when ex-ante risk rises**. The premiums predict ex-post VIX-futures returns with a coefficient near one, and falling premiums predict rising market and investment risk. The proposed mechanism is that hedgers cash out and do not re-buy. A replication describes the premium as possibly turning negative in severe stress; that wording comes from the replicator, not the paper. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i1p180-227..html); [Tuck summary](https://www.tuck.dartmouth.edu/news/articles/the-puzzling-market-for-stock-market-volatility-insurance); [ISCTE replication](https://www.repositorio-iul.iscte.pt/handle/10071/24158)
- **Own computation (VIX/VIX3M, 2006-07 → 2026-10; 152 inversion runs, 554 inverted days)**:
  - 20-day forward mean log return by ratio bucket: <0.85: +0.48%; 0.85–0.90: +0.71%; 0.90–0.95: +0.68%; 0.95–1.00: +0.89%; 1.00–1.05: +1.60% (n=328); 1.05–1.10: +4.00% (n=93, P(up) 80.6%); **>1.10: −2.26% (n=133, 18 episodes), median +1.27%, 10th percentile −21.3%**.
  - Mean forward 20-day realized vol / mean VIX by bucket is about 0.76–0.83 for every ratio up to 1.10, but **1.18 for ratio > 1.10** (51.2% realized vs 43.5% VIX). At 5 days it is 55.8/43.5 ≈ 1.28.
  - **Within VIX ≥ 25**, inverted days averaged +1.02% over 20 days versus +1.98% for non-inverted days, so inversion added no bullish information beyond VIX level.
  - Data: [Yahoo ^VIX3M](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX3M/)

### Inferences
- Read the VIX/VIX3M ratio mainly as a **regime and VRP-size gauge**. In mild inversion, implied vol remains a (somewhat) upward-biased forecast of realized vol. In extreme inversion (>1.1), the bias flips: realized has exceeded VIX, so the implied cone was *too narrow*. This agrees with Cheng (the premium shrinks under stress) and with Johnson (an inverted slope means variance assets are cheap relative to future payoffs).
- **Folklore flag:** "inversion marks the bottom" is mixed. Moderate backwardation was followed by good average returns, but the deepest inversions contained the crash continuations (2008-09, 2020-02). The positive median combined with the very negative mean and P10 shows a bimodal outcome: snap-back or cascade.
- A short-end ratio (VIX9D/VIX or 1-week ATM IV vs 1-month) would capture event-driven inversion more directly for a 1–20-day monitor. It is untested here.

### Gaps
- The published JFQA (2017) coefficient magnitudes and any out-of-sample results were not retrieved. Only the abstract, plus the 2011 equity-return draft, was available.
- Fassas & Hourvouliades (VIX futures term structure) was not retrieved.
- No peer-reviewed study of VIX/VIX3M (as opposed to the VIX-futures basis) and 1–20-day index returns was found. The own computation above is in-sample with 18 extreme episodes.

## Q3. Variance risk premium (BTZ 2009; Drechsler-Yaron 2011; Bekaert-Hoerova 2014): horizon, daily implementation, out-of-sample record

### Takeaway
VRP (VIX² minus expected or realized variance) is the best-documented options-based return predictor, but it works at **about one quarter**: R² is roughly 3–7% at the quarterly horizon and small at one month. Using a better (HAR-type) physical-variance forecast *reduces* the measured predictability (Bekaert-Hoerova). Out-of-sample evidence ranges from "comparably weak" to positive only with conditioning (Pyun). For 1–20-day horizons, VRP is better used to **convert the risk-neutral cone into a physical one** than as a direction signal.

### Cited Findings
- Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009, *RFS* 22(11):4463–4492). VRP is implied minus realized variation. It explains "a nontrivial fraction" of post-1990 aggregate return variation, is strongest at the **quarterly** horizon, and outperforms P/E, the default spread and cay there. The 2007 working draft (1990–2005) reports **>15% of quarterly excess-return variation**, and >25% combined with P/E. The published numbers may differ. — [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/732839); [CREATES WP](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/08/rp08_48.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=948309)
- Drechsler & Yaron (2011, *RFS* 24(1):1–45) define the variance premium as VIX² minus *expected* realized variance from a time-series model. They report "large and statistically significant" return predictability concentrated at **short horizons measured in months**, and link the premium to uncertainty about fundamentals through a long-run-risks model. The working paper notes that results depend on model-free implied variance and high-frequency realized variance. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v24y2011i1p1-45.html); [NBER SI 2008 draft](https://img.nber.org/conferences/2008/si2008/EFEL/yaron.pdf)
- Bekaert & Hoerova (2014, *J. Econometrics* 183:181–192; NBER w18995). Data are daily from Jan 1990 to Oct 2010 and the regressions monthly; they run an out-of-sample horse race of volatility models with a 1990–Jul 2005 estimation window. The winners are **Corsi HAR-type realized-variance models** (models 8, 10, 14).
  - The BTZ martingale VRP (VIX² − past RV) predicts returns at 1, 3 and 12 months, strongest at quarterly. With the best-model VRP, **only the quarterly horizon is significant and R² drops from 7% to 3–5%**: "results in BTZ exaggerate the predictive power".
  - VRP still "remains a reliable predictor," and is *stronger* once 2008–2010 is added.
  - Conditional variance does **not** predict returns, but it does predict economic activity (negatively, significant at 1%) and is more correlated with financial instability.
  - With dividend yield and credit spread added, adjusted R² is "small at the one month horizon" but 12–20% quarterly and about 27% annual. The authors warn this "may partially reflect statistical bias."
  — [NBER w18995 PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18995.pdf); [Columbia abstract](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/vix-variance-premium-and-stock-market-volatility)
- Pyun (2019, *JFE* 132:150–174). Working-paper text: BTZ's VRP predicts returns up to six months in-sample, but "the out-of-sample performance is comparably weak." His beta-conditioned method (scaling the VRP signal by the contemporaneous return-on-variance-innovation slope) gives a **monthly out-of-sample R² of ~8%** and +0.1 annual Sharpe. These are working-paper figures, not verified in the published version. Predictions are best when returns are highly correlated with variance shocks. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v132y2019i1p150-174.html); [AEA 2017 WP](https://swlb1.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/GDde2yiy); [Fed IFDP WP](https://fedinprint.org/item/fedgfe/34100/original)
- Bollerslev, Marrone, Xu & Zhou (*JFQA* 2014) revisit inference with overlapping regressions and add international evidence. Results were not retrieved. — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/stock-return-predictability-and-variance-risk-premia-statistical-inference-and-international-evidence/0BE5DE1D942A0342DDBA24D7BFBEA5C8)
- Bollerslev, Todorov & Xu (2015, *JFE* 118(1):113–134): much of VRP's return predictability comes from its **jump-tail** component, the compensation for left-tail jump risk ("market fears"). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v118y2015i1p113-134.html); [CREATES WP](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/14/rp14_49.pdf)
- **Own computation**: over 1990–2026, mean VIX² − forward-21-day RV² is +110 variance points. Mean VIX is 19.44 against mean forward realized vol of 15.38, with median RV/VIX = 0.73. — data: [Yahoo ^VIX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX/)

### Inferences
- **Daily implementation recipe (deterministic):** VRP_t = VIX_t²/100² × (30/365) − E_t[RV over the next 30 calendar days]. Fit a HAR(1, 5, 22) on daily RV with expanding or rolling OLS, refit weekly. Close-to-close squared returns work if no intraday data are available; Bekaert-Hoerova's winners used realized/bipower variation. Report the VRP z-score against a 1–3-year window.
- Label VRP's horizon honestly ("~quarterly evidence; weak at 1–20 days"). Its 1–20-day use is to set the size of the Q→P haircut on the cone.
- Because the BTZ "VIX² − past RV" version overstates predictability compared with a forward-looking forecast, use the HAR version.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests VRP specifically at 1–20-*trading-day* horizons out-of-sample.
- Exact published BTZ R² tables were not retrieved.

## Q4. VVIX, SKEW, risk-neutral skewness and tail-risk measures (Park; Bakshi-Kapadia-Madan; Conrad-Dittmar-Ghysels; Kelly-Jiang; Bollerslev-Todorov)

### Takeaway
These measures mostly predict the **pricing or returns of tail hedges** and **longer-horizon (monthly to annual) premia**, not 1–20-day index direction. VVIX forecasts lower returns on tail hedges over 3–4 weeks. The raw Cboe SKEW index is widely regarded as a poor crash timer, and the claim that it signals crashes is industry folklore. A downside-only decomposition (Bevilacqua-Tunaru) reportedly predicts downturns out-of-sample. Kelly-Jiang tail risk predicts *higher* future returns at monthly to annual horizons. Skew and variance premia share one factor (Kozhan-Neuberger-Schneider), so their information overlaps.

### Cited Findings
- Park (Fed FEDS 2013-54; published as Park 2015, reportedly *J. Financial Markets* 26:38–63, "Volatility-of-volatility and tail risk hedging returns"; the journal is unverified, and the task's "J. Futures Markets" citation could not be confirmed). VVIX is a model-free risk-neutral vol-of-vol built from VIX options. Higher VVIX coincides with more expensive tail hedges (S&P puts, VIX calls), which then earn **lower returns over the next 3–4 weeks**. The predictive power comes mainly from integrated vol-of-vol, not volatility jumps. — [Fed FEDS 2013-54 abstract](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201354/201354abs.html); [Fed PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201354/201354pap.pdf); [journal citation via industry doc page](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/documentation/vvix)
- Cboe SKEW: built from deep-OTM SPX options; 100 means a normal distribution. Industry sources say it "prices tails rather than predicting them," rises when protection is cheap and demanded in calm markets, and has a level that has drifted structurally (about 115 in the 1990s, 140–150s more recently). **These are industry or blog sources, and the underlying academic studies were not located.** — [LuxAlgo (industry)](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/skew-index/); [Seeking Alpha (blog)](https://seekingalpha.com/instablog/181578-elusive-value/5603561-skew-is-extremely-elevated-level); [FXStreet (industry)](https://www.fxstreet.com/amp/analysis/could-excessive-hedging-sink-the-sp-500-201710111438)
- Bevilacqua & Tunaru (2021, *J. Financial Stability* 53:100816, "The SKEW index: Extracting what has been left") split SKEW into an upside (call) part, which tracks sentiment, and a downside (put) part, which tracks existing tail-risk measures. The **negative SKEW predicts recessions, market downturns and uncertainty up to one year ahead**, robust to other tail measures and **out-of-sample**. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/108198); [Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/id/eprint/3179596)
- Gambarelli & Muzzioli (EU risk-asymmetry indices): only a RAX-type aggregate asymmetry index forecast future negative returns in all 12 EU markets, and only at very high levels. — [UNIMORE WP](https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1207394)
- Kozhan, Neuberger & Schneider (2013, *RFS* 26(9):2174–2203). A skew swap (implied vs realized skew) shows the skew premium accounts for **>40% of the slope of the S&P 500 implied-vol curve**. Strategies that capture skew while hedging variance, or the reverse, earn an insignificant premium, so a single factor drives both. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v26y2013i9p2174-2203.html); [City accepted version](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/15209/); [CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/11/the-skew-risk-premium-in-the-equity-index-market-digest-summary)
- Kelly & Jiang (2014, *RFS* 27(10):2841–2871). Monthly tail risk estimated from the *cross-section* of firm-level crashes (not options) has strong predictive power for market returns. NBER WP: **+1 SD of tail risk → +4.5% excess market return over the next year**. A later paper reports the measure works at 1-month to 5-year horizons. It correlates significantly with S&P 500 option-implied tail measures and predicts weaker real activity. In the cross-section, high-tail-beta stocks earn **+5.4%/yr** three-factor alpha. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v27y2014i10p2841-2871..html); [NBER w19375](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19375.pdf)
- Bollerslev, Todorov & Xu (2015, *JFE*): the jump-tail part of VRP drives the return predictability. A citing paper says *negative*, not positive, tail risk is priced (secondary). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v118y2015i1p113-134.html); [NYU Stern citing paper](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Chow%20Li%20Sopranzetti-The%20Predictive%20Power%20of%20Tail%20Risk%20Premia%20on%20Individual%20Stock%20Returns.pdf)
- Bekaert & Hoerova's reference list includes Bakshi, Kapadia & Madan (2003, *RFS*, "Stock Return Characteristics, Skew Laws, and the Differential Pricing of Individual Equity Options"), the standard model-free risk-neutral-skewness estimator. Its index-level predictive results were not retrieved. — [NBER w18995 references](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18995.pdf)

### Inferences
- VVIX belongs on the monitor as a "**cost of crash insurance**" gauge (high VVIX means hedges are expensive and have historically underperformed over the next 3–4 weeks). It should not be presented as an index-direction signal.
- SKEW should be either dropped or labelled "prices tails, not a crash timer," given its level drift and the lack of peer-reviewed timing evidence. A downside-only skew (OTM-put-only risk-neutral skewness, or the 25Δ put − ATM IV spread) is the better-supported variant and can be computed from the same SPY chain.
- Given Kozhan-Neuberger-Schneider, skew and VRP should not be scored as independent votes in any composite, because that double-counts one factor.

### Gaps
- No primary evidence was retrieved for Conrad-Dittmar-Ghysels (2013), which is mainly cross-sectional, or for index-level risk-neutral skewness predicting 1–20-day returns.
- No peer-reviewed source was found that directly shows the Cboe SKEW index has *no* predictive power. The "weak predictor" view rests on industry sources.
- Park's journal publication details are unverified.

## Q5. Risk-neutral vs physical: converting an options-implied cone to real-world probabilities; ±1σ exceedance; bias by VIX regime

### Takeaway
Risk-neutral (Q) distributions are **systematically too wide and too left-heavy** for physical outcomes on average. A power-utility (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou) or empirical risk transformation produces densities that pass forecast-calibration tests at 1-, 2- and 4-week horizons (Shackleton-Taylor-Yu), whereas Ross recovery fails empirically. In our data, a VIX-scaled ±1σ band was breached on only **15–22%** of windows, against 31.7% nominal. The bias is **regime-dependent**: the band is too wide in calm regimes, about right at 5–20 days in extreme regimes, and **too narrow at 1 day after crashes or when VIX ≥ 40**.

### Inferences
- **The user's straddle cone is narrower than the VIX cone measured above.** An ATM straddle prices roughly the risk-neutral *mean absolute move*. With negative skew and fat tails, ATM IV sits below the variance-swap rate (VIX), so a straddle-σ cone is closer to physical width than a VIX-σ cone. The over-width figures above are therefore an **upper bound** on the straddle cone's over-width. The monitor must be calibrated on its *own* logged cone.
- Mathematical check on `√(π/2)`, own derivation: for a fixed mean absolute deviation, the 10/90 quantiles of a normal (1.606·MAD) and of a Laplace (b·ln 5 = 1.609·MAD) are nearly identical. **P10–P90 built from the straddle are therefore robust to tail shape, but P1/P5/P95/P99 are not**: Laplace P99 = 3.91·MAD vs normal 2.92·MAD. Extending the cone beyond P10/P90 needs wing information (OTM puts and calls, e.g., SVI).
- The bias is **regime- and horizon-dependent, not a constant haircut**:
  - In calm or normal regimes, the cone is about 25% too wide (SD z ≈ 0.75).
  - In extreme regimes the 5–20-day cone is roughly right or slightly wide, but the **1-day cone is too narrow** (SD z 1.16–1.26). This is consistent with Cheng (the premium falls when risk rises) and with backwardation (short-dated IV > 30-day VIX). The 1-day test above uses 30-day VIX, so a true 1-day straddle should do better.
  - **Asymmetry**: in normal times, downside misses (P10) outnumber upside misses (P90) by about 1.5× at 5–20 days, reflecting physical negative skew under a symmetric-lognormal cone. Right after sharp drops the upside misses dominate at 1–5 days (rebound volatility). After deep drawdowns the 20-day downside misses dominate again (cascade risk).
- Weekend and holiday treatment matters for 1-day cones. Calendar-day variance interpolation assigns weekend days full variance, which inflates Monday cones. Trading-time or event-weighted variance is more accurate. This is an inference; supporting literature (e.g., French-Roll on weekend variance) was not verified this session.

### Gaps
- The Christoffersen-Jacobs work and the Bliss-Panigirtzoglou published horizon tables were not retrieved; the risk-aversion-by-horizon numbers are secondary.
- No source gives historical SPY *ATM-straddle* implied move vs realized coverage. The computation above uses VIX as a proxy and needs to be repeated on the monitor's own straddle history (not available via yfinance).
- "Conditional frequency realized > implied given high VIX" in the peer-reviewed literature: not found. A claimed 85.1% unconditional figure from gereview.onrender.com is unverifiable and excluded. Our own 85.3% (100% − 14.7%) matches it.

## Q6. Practical: how often realized exceeds the straddle-implied move after selloffs; should the Selloff Monitor show a VRP-adjusted cone; concrete upgrades

### Takeaway
After selloffs, realized moves exceed the implied move **more often than in normal times**. Forward 21-day RV > VIX on 20–27% of post-selloff days vs 14.7% overall, and 1-day moves exceed ±1σ on 44% of days after a ≤ −5% week. They still exceed it *less often* than the 31.7% the lognormal implies at 5–20 days. **Yes, show a VRP-adjusted (physical) cone**, but as a second layer next to the Q cone. Its shrink factor should be regime- and horizon-dependent, tilted for skew and drift, and validated by the monitor's own rolling coverage log. In extreme backwardation or right after crashes, **do not shrink** (k ≥ 1 at 1–5 days).

### Cited Findings
- Post-selloff exceedance rates, own computation (see Q5 for the full table): RV21 > VIX on 27.1% of days after a ≤ −5% 5-day drop and 20.1–20.5% after a drawdown of ≤ −10% or ≤ −20%, vs 14.7% unconditional. Median RV/VIX is 0.84 after sharp drops vs 0.73 unconditional. With VIX/VIX3M > 1.1, mean 20-day RV/VIX is 1.18. — data: [Yahoo ^VIX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX/), [Yahoo ^GSPC](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EGSPC/), [Yahoo ^VIX3M](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EVIX3M/)
- Literature support for regime dependence: VIX-futures premiums fall when risk rises (Cheng 2019) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i1p180-227..html); the VIX overshoot is consistent "except during spike periods" (CFA) — [CFA Enterprising Investor](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2024/07/31/how-well-does-the-market-predict-volatility); an inverted VIX slope predicts higher variance-asset returns, i.e., a smaller VRP (Johnson 2017) — [JFQA abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/risk-premia-and-the-vix-term-structure/56572D1F060448571BD8F597C732D9C3)
- Literature support for transforming Q into P: risk-adjusted densities are unbiased (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou) — [Chicago Fed WP](https://www.chicagofed.org/~/media/publications/working-papers/2001/wp2001-15r3-pdf.pdf); risk-transformed densities pass at 1–4 weeks (Shackleton-Taylor-Yu) — [EFMA 2006](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2006-Madrid/papers/627120_full.pdf); Ross recovery fails while power utility and historical benchmarks survive (Jackwerth-Menner) — [AEA WP](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/yNn4GdY9)
- HAR-type models win the out-of-sample physical-variance horse race (Bekaert-Hoerova). — [NBER w18995](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18995.pdf)

### Inferences — concrete, deterministic upgrades for the Selloff Monitor
1. **Two cones, clearly labelled.** Keep the current straddle cone as the *"Options-implied (risk-neutral)"* cone. Add a *"Real-world (VRP-adjusted)"* cone:
   - σ_P(h) = k(h, regime) · σ_Q(h), with median shifted up by an empirical drift μ_P(h) relative to the Q median of −σ²/2. Own data: 20-day median z ≈ +0.25σ unconditional.
   - Seed k from the VIX-proxy medians above (≈0.73–0.77 in calm or normal regimes; ≈0.84 when VIX ≥ 40 or after a ≤ −5% week; ≥1.0–1.2 at 1–5 days when VIX/VIX3M > 1.1). Then **re-estimate k from the monitor's own logged straddle cones**, because straddle-σ < VIX-σ means the right k for straddles is closer to 1.
2. **Alternative P-variance, more defensible:** σ²_P = w·k·σ²_Q + (1−w)·HAR-RV forecast, with w fitted by rolling out-of-sample log-score or CRPS. This is the Bekaert-Hoerova recipe for E^P[variance] and keeps the cone deterministic and reconcilable.
3. **Self-calibration panel (PIT log).** Store each day's cone quantiles per horizon (1/5/10/20 days) and the realized outcome. Display rolling coverage, e.g. "last 250 obs: 4% below P10, 3% above P90, SD z 0.78," split by regime. This makes the cone's honesty measurable, matching the project's math-accuracy standard. A Berkowitz test on the PIT series is the standard check (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou).
4. **Skewed, not symmetric, P-cone.** Lognormal symmetry under-predicts downside misses in normal times and upside misses after crashes. Shape the cone from the SPY smile, either the SVI/RND engine already in `quant_service` or the 25Δ risk reversal. Then apply a power-utility tilt (Bliss-Panigirtzoglou; γ higher at shorter horizons) or an empirical PIT→quantile isotonic map fitted per regime.
5. **Keep P10–P90 straddle-only; don't extend to P1/P5 without wings.** The `√(π/2)` MAD→σ mapping is robust at the 10/90 quantiles but understates the 1/99 tails under fat-tailed distributions.
6. **Term-structure tile reframed as a regime and VRP gauge.** Suggested labels: VIX/VIX3M < 0.95 "normal"; 0.95–1.0 "elevated"; 1.0–1.1 "backwardation: historically above-average forward mean, wider range"; > 1.1 "**extreme: realized vol has exceeded implied; left tail fat**". Add VIX9D/VIX or the 1-week vs 1-month straddle IV ratio for event-driven short-end inversions. Do *not* call any state a buy signal: within VIX ≥ 25, inversion added nothing over the VIX level.
7. **VRP tile:** VIX² − HAR forecast in variance points with a percentile. Caption: "predicts ~quarterly returns (R² 3–7% in the literature); weak at 1–20 days; used here to size the real-world cone."
8. **VVIX tile captioned as "price of crash insurance"** (Park: high VVIX → poor tail-hedge returns over 3–4 weeks). **SKEW**: drop it, or caption it "prices tails; not a crash timer (folklore)". Prefer a downside-only skew (25Δ put IV − ATM IV) computed from the same SPY chain. Do not count skew and VRP as independent signals (Kozhan-Neuberger-Schneider).
9. **Analog fan honesty.** Condition analogs on *speed* (5-day return) and *depth* (drawdown) separately, because their 20-day outcomes differ: +2.5% vs +0.3% mean. Show the **number of independent episodes**, not days (e.g., "13 episodes since 1990" for VIX ≥ 40; "46" for ≤ −5% weeks; "18" for VIX/VIX3M > 1.1). Prefer the median plus P10/P90 over the mean, since the extreme-inversion mean (−2.3%) and median (+1.3%) disagree in sign.
10. **Calendar vs trading time.** For 1-day and 5-day cones, consider trading-day variance with event-day weights (FOMC, CPI, NFP) instead of plain calendar-day interpolation, which inflates Monday and holiday cones.
11. **MOVE:** keep as context only. No peer-reviewed short-horizon equity-return evidence was found in this session.

### Gaps
- The right k for *straddle-derived* (not VIX-derived) σ is unknown until the monitor logs its own history. SPY ATM straddle history is not available through yfinance, and OptionMetrics is the usual academic source.
- No out-of-sample study was found that compares a VRP-scaled cone with a raw implied cone at 1–20 days specifically. Shackleton-Taylor-Yu (1991–2004) is the closest, and its full text was not retrievable (HTTP 503).
- All own-computation statistics are in-sample, use overlapping windows, and are clustered in a small number of stress episodes. Confidence intervals were not computed. Effective sample sizes are roughly the episode counts given, not the day counts.
